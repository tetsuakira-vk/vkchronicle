After two years of silence, legendary Japanese rock act GLAY has returned with *Tabisuru Riyuu wo Machingara* (Waiting for a Reason to Travel), their 18th studio album and the first major statement since completing their landmark 30th anniversary celebrations.

For those unfamiliar: GLAY emerged from the Visual Kei underground in the mid-1990s to become one of Japan's biggest rock exports. With their cinematic production, anthemic songwriting, and theatrical presentation, they've maintained a devoted global fanbase across three decades. This album arrives as a philosophical reset—a moment where leader TAKURO and the band have deliberately questioned their own working methods.

![GLAY 18th Album Cover](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fvijuttoke.com%2Fcontents%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2FGLAY_18thAL_JKT_s-580x580.jpg&w=800&output=jpg)

Traditionally, GLAY would write material, record it, then debut songs on tour. This time, they've reversed the process entirely—composing, rehearsing in the studio, and road-testing tracks live before capturing the final versions. TAKURO's rationale is refreshingly honest: in a rapidly shifting entertainment landscape, the band needed to rediscover what actually matters to them rather than default to old formulas.

The 15-track album spans impressive stylistic ground. Guitarist HISASHI contributes the gritty, mixtuire-heavy "Kitanakatta Eiyuu" (Soiled Hero), while bassist JIRO provides the title track—a pop-oriented centerpiece that gives the album its thematic anchor. Track listing includes standouts like "EXOFIRE," "INAZUMA," and a striking collaboration between TAKURO and singer Shoji Oda on "Higanzakura."

Available in three formats—G-Direct limited edition with CD/Blu-ray (¥12,100), standard CD+DVD (¥6,710), and CD-only (¥3,850)—the deluxe version includes live footage from January's Lawson 50th Anniversary show and special video content featuring voice actors from the anime *Ace of Diamond*.

GLAY kicks off their "EXOFIRE" Arena Tour in November across all major Japanese cities through February 2027.
