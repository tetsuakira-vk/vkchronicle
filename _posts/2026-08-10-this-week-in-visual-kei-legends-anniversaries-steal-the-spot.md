What a week for Visual Kei nostalgia and milestone celebrations. From long-running acts marking decades in the scene to beloved legends making unexpected moves, this has been the kind of VK news cycle that reminds us why we're so invested in this community.

Let's start with the anniversary energy. DEZERT kicked things off by marking 15 years with "Alternate4," a venue-exclusive release that's already got fans hunting for tour schedules—because nothing says loyalty like limited-edition physical releases at live shows. Meanwhile, Chisato of PENICILLIN announced a massive solo 30th anniversary project following Crack6's completion of their "Insane Silentia" tour, proving that even after three decades in the game, the drive to create is undiminished. KISAKI also returned to headlines with a second memorial album and a Tokyo encore show that has the symphonic/gothic crowd absolutely buzzing.

On the singles and album front, ASAGI dropped "Tenrinzoku" and announced a birthday finale live, while the surprise of the week has to be Creamy Mami's cover album of Pierrot's magical girl classics—yes, really. It's J-Pop meets Visual Kei nostalgia, and the 2026 release window means we're still waiting, but it's the kind of left-field project that makes you remember why VK artists are constantly pushing boundaries.

Live reports painted a fuller picture this week. Versailles reflected on their return to the stage with the kind of thoughtful perspective you'd expect from a symphonic metal institution, while the Kyushu Biteki Matsuri finals traveled to Fukuoka and Kumamoto, giving us solid on-the-ground coverage of the regional scene that often flies under international radar. And if you haven't caught 鬼堕くん。 yet, their interview about raw emotion and unfiltered studio work is worth your time—experimental Visual Kei is alive and well.

What stands out is the mix: established acts proving their staying power, solo artists claiming their moment, and the regional touring circuit keeping grass-roots VK thriving. This is a healthy ecosystem.

Keep your eyes on tour announcements related to those venue-exclusive releases and Chisato's anniversary project—these things tend to expand beyond their initial rollouts once demand becomes clear.
