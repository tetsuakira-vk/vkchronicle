After nearly four years in hiatus, Angelo returned to live performance last October and have been building momentum ever since. The Japanese Visual Kei quintet—fronted by the commanding presence of vocalist Kirito alongside guitarists Karyu and Giru, bassist KOHTA, and drummer TAKEO—just wrapped their 20th anniversary tour *THE TRINITY* with a sold-out homecoming performance that felt less like a victory lap and more like a spiritual rebirth.

On October 4th at Omiya Sonic City in Saitama, Angelo reclaimed what they call "Angel's Day"—traditionally their anniversary concert date. The significance wasn't lost on anyone in the room: the last time they played this venue on this date was 2020, during the height of COVID restrictions when capacity was slashed to barely half. This time, the hall ran at full power, and so did the band.

![Angelo performs at Omiya Sonic City](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnew-img.barks.jp%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F10%2F05134124%2FAngelo%25E5%25AF%25841-683x1024.jpg&w=800&output=jpg)

Across 24 songs spanning their catalog—from bruising openers like "FAITH" and their latest track "NEOCREATE" through emotionally layered mid-set passages and into an absolutely ferocious encore—Angelo delivered exactly what their reunion demanded: proof that they hadn't simply survived the gap years, but had transformed because of them.

The night's emotional centerpiece came in Kirito's final remarks, where he spoke candidly about living without guarantees. "Tomorrow isn't promised," he told the crowd. "That's what makes right now precious. Stay true to yourselves—stay that beautiful, crazy version of who you are—and maybe we'll get another miracle." It's the kind of philosophy that defines Angelo's entire artistic identity: aggressive, unflinching, and deeply human beneath the theatrical darkness.

The *THE TRINITY* tour title refers both to the three dates and to the concept of past, present, and future woven into one narrative thread. It's a framework that makes sense when you consider how Angelo has always operated—as a band unafraid to ask uncomfortable questions and demand transformation, both from themselves and their audience.

![Angelo mid-performance at Omiya](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnew-img.barks.jp%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F10%2F05134153%2FAngelo%25E5%25AF%25842-683x1024.jpg&w=800&output=jpg)

For fans who missed the tour, Angelo released the best-of album *ECLIPSE* in September—a symbolic title, since eclipses traditionally represent rebirth and new cycles beginning. It's the perfect companion piece to what this October night represented: not an ending, but an eclipse in the truest sense.
