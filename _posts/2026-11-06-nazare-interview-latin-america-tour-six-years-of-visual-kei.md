NAZARE sat down ahead of their São Paulo performance to discuss their trajectory in visual kei and what the band expects from their Latin American expansion—a significant milestone for any Japanese rock act breaking into Western markets.

For those new to NAZARE: the band has spent six years building their theatrical aesthetic and sonic identity within the visual kei underground. Like their peers in the scene, they combine elaborate costumes, dramatic makeup, and androgynous presentation with hard rock instrumentation. Their approach mirrors the genre's commitment to performance art as integral to the music itself—a philosophy that translates across language and cultural barriers, which is precisely why visual kei has gained traction among international fans over the past decade.

The timing of this Latin American tour reflects growing momentum for Japanese visual kei outside Japan. Territories like Brazil have developed dedicated fan communities hungry for live experiences with bands they've previously only encountered through streaming and social media. For NAZARE, a six-year-old band, touring South America represents validation that their visual identity and musical output resonate beyond domestic audiences.

The interview covered their career arc, their relationship to visual kei as a movement, and how they're approaching these new markets. These are the conversations that matter—not just promotional soundbites, but genuine insight into how bands navigate international touring, adapt their performances for different audiences, and stay true to their aesthetic vision while expanding their reach.

Latin America's visual kei fanbase has quietly grown into something substantial. Bands like Dir En Grey, the GazettE, and Malice Mizer have cultivated dedicated followings there, but opportunities for emerging acts to tour the region remain relatively rare. NAZARE's willingness to invest in these markets speaks to their ambition and their faith in the universal appeal of visual kei's theatrical ethos.
