What a week to be a Visual Kei fan. November's been building momentum all month, and this stretch delivered the kind of news cycle that reminds us why the scene remains so vital—established legends stepping up, emerging acts breaking through, and the kind of creative reinvention that keeps VK perpetually fresh.

The comeback energy was real. JET's return after a nine-year hiatus with the 'Get Born' Comeback Tour is legitimately huge; that's the kind of reunion that had fans wondering if it would ever happen, so seeing the band re-energize feels significant. Meanwhile, KIRITO's launching the OVERRIDE Tour, positioning it as a new chapter—always a signal that something transformative is brewing behind the scenes.

Anniversaries hit hard this week too. ASAGI's milestone 20-year solo career celebration feels almost monumental: dual albums with 55 guest musicians is the kind of ambitious, collaborative statement that only an artist with that kind of legacy and reach could pull off. ViVaRush's countdown to their 10th-anniversary final with a 24-hour livestream challenge shows how the band's pushing creative boundaries right up to the end—very on-brand for them.

On the live front, 黒蜜 sold out Shibuya REX and immediately announced a second national tour. That's the kind of rapid-fire momentum that marks a band genuinely breaking through; they're not waiting to see if interest holds—they're capitalizing on it. Meanwhile, fuzzy knot is celebrating Shinji's birthday with a live at Kinema Club, keeping the smaller, intimate show tradition alive alongside all the bigger announcements.

The digital side's thriving too. Azavana's rolling out a member documentary series on YouTube, w.o.d. is releasing lyric videos for all 20 tracks from 'Fureru,' and new artist Pastel Closet just dropped their debut CD "Eternal Startline." It's a good reminder that Visual Kei's ecosystem spans everything from grassroots bedroom projects to arena-level operations, and this week captured that spectrum perfectly.

Plastic Tree's Arimura Ryutaro releasing his first solo album in eight years was quietly one of the week's most anticipated drops—there's always intrigue around what established guitarists do when they step outside their main project.

It's been an unusually dense week of announcements and releases. Keep your eyes on early December for how these tours shake out and whether any of these "new chapter" moments deliver the sonic pivots we're anticipating.
