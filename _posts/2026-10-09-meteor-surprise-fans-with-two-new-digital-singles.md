In a move that's sure to delight long-dormant fans, Visual Kei veterans Meteor have emerged from the vault with two brand-new digital singles. While the band called it quits back in 2008, they've spent the past year gradually resurrecting their legacy on streaming platforms—and now they're giving supporters even more reasons to revisit the project.

For those unfamiliar with Meteor, the group was a fixture of the mid-2000s Visual Kei scene, bringing a distinctive sonic identity to an era defined by experimentation and theatrical presentation. Though their active run was relatively brief, their impact on underground VK circles remained significant enough that fans have long held out hope for some form of return. Last year's decision to digitize their back catalog felt like a modest acknowledgment of that enduring support. This latest move takes things a step further.

The two new singles represent material that sits outside Meteor's original discography—fresh content rather than archival deep cuts. It's a meaningful distinction. This isn't simply about making old recordings accessible; it's about the band actively contributing to the conversation again, even if in limited capacity. The decision to roll out new work gradually through digital platforms aligns with how many dormant acts are choosing to re-engage with their fanbases in 2024: low-pressure, streaming-first, and thoughtfully paced.

What remains unclear is whether these singles signal a broader reunion or simply a one-off gesture. Either way, the move demonstrates that Meteor hasn't entirely closed the door on their legacy—and for fans who've spent sixteen years hoping for some acknowledgment, that's substantial.

Head to your preferred streaming service to check out the new singles. Spotify, Apple Music, Bandcamp, and YouTube Music all carry Visual Kei releases, and these tracks should be discoverable through standard searches for the band's name.
