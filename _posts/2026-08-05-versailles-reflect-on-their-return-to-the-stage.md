Versailles are back, and they've got stories to tell. Ahead of their appearance at B7KLAN J-Rock Festival, the legendary symphonic metal outfit sat down to discuss what brought them back to performing, their deep ties to France, and the weight of returning to stages after time away.

For those unfamiliar, Versailles are one of Visual Kei's most ambitious acts — a five-piece ensemble that fuses ornate gothic aesthetics with classically-influenced metal arrangements. Fronted by the commanding Kamijo, the band has long positioned themselves as Visual Kei's answer to European symphonic metal, complete with baroque instrumentation, intricate layering, and visual presentations that rival anything in the scene. Their name itself reflects their aesthetic philosophy: grandeur, opulence, and European romanticism filtered through a distinctly Japanese sensibility.

The band's relationship with France runs surprisingly deep, and that cultural bridge clearly matters to them beyond surface-level aesthetics. In the interview, they reflect on how European influences have shaped their music and image, and what it means to carry that torch while remaining rooted in the Japanese underground. There's also an underlying current of emotion in their remarks — the feeling of stepping back into live performance after absence carries weight that only a band with Versailles' theatrical DNA can fully articulate.

What makes this interview particularly interesting is the timing. The B7KLAN J-Rock Festival represents a significant moment for the band, one where they're positioned alongside other major acts in the Japanese rock landscape. Their willingness to discuss their history and artistic philosophy in detail suggests they're approaching this return with intention and reflection, not mere nostalgia.

For longtime Versailles devotees and newcomers alike, this is essential reading. The band remains one of Visual Kei's most musically sophisticated acts, and hearing them discuss their creative journey directly offers rare insight into what drives them.
