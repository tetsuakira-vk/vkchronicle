When a Visual Kei band throws a birthday party for its frontman, you know it's going to be something special—and LM.C's annual maya BIRTHDAY LIVE 2026 "Buck Moon" on July 30th didn't disappoint. The veteran Tokyo outfit pulled out all the stops to mark the occasion in style, treating fans to a carefully curated setlist that balanced deep cuts with party anthems, all wrapped up in the kind of theatrical celebration only a VK band can deliver.

The "Buck Moon" title carries its own significance: it's the name given to July's full moon in Native American tradition, believed to carry energies of transformation, rebirth, and renewed vitality. With the actual full moon having peaked just hours before the show, the symbolism wasn't lost on an audience gathered to celebrate a band now approaching its 20th anniversary this autumn.

![LM.C performs at the Buck Moon birthday live](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnew-img.barks.jp%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F08%2F13151413%2F01_LMC_1-1024x683.jpg&w=800&output=jpg)

The night kicked off with Aiji's solid guitar work on the instrumental "Be STRONG, Be POP," before maya burst onto stage to lead the crowd into "The BUDDHA"—a track that encapsulates LM.C's master plan both sonically and philosophically. Rather than building gradually, the band hit full throttle from the opening seconds, setting an energetic tone that never let up.

Setlist highlights included unexpected treats like "Bell the CAT," "YASHA姫," and "EDO FUNK," tracks that don't see regular rotation in most LM.C shows. The pairing of "YASHA姫" and "EDO FUNK"—both summer-themed numbers—showed real thoughtfulness in the arrangement. During "EDO FUNK," maya cleverly rewrote the lyric "絶好の夏祭り日和" (perfect summer festival weather) as "絶好のお誕生日日和" (perfect birthday weather), a flourish that perfectly captured the spirit of the evening.

![Maya performs during the birthday special](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnew-img.barks.jp%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F08%2F13151543%2F01_maya_1-1024x683.jpg&w=800&output=jpg)

The between-song banter was quintessential maya—self-deprecating humor mixed with genuine gratitude. Two surprise birthday cakes appeared during the show (the second presented as a light-up cake, though technical difficulties meant it stayed dark—"Aiji prepared this!" maya joked). By the final stretch, the crowd was singing along to "It's a Wonderful Wonder World," clearly riding the wave of collective happiness radiating from the stage.

For a band that's weathered the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake, the COVID pandemic, and the tumultuous shifts in global affairs that 2026 has brought, this show felt like a genuine celebration of resilience. That "ordinary" moments—gathering together, celebrating another year—have become genuinely precious is a truth LM.C's anniversary year embodies perfectly.
