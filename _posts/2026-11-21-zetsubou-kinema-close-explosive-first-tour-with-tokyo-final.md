In less than a year, Zetsubou Kinema has gone from debut to a full three-city tour culminating in a packed house at Tokyo's Shibuya WOMB—a trajectory that speaks volumes about the five-piece's magnetic pull on audiences hungry for something genuinely unhinged.

Launched in December 2024, the Nagoya-based unit channels the dark, introverted energy of their hometown's legendary scene while wrapping it in contemporary visual storytelling and visceral performance. Their conceptual framework—"tragedy as comedy"—isn't mere posturing. On October 13, they proved it during the final show of their "Sorrowful Peepshow Rhetoric" tour, an 80-minute non-stop assault of theatrical rock that felt more like witnessing a fever dream than attending a typical live show.

The stage itself was a character: crimson curtains, wooden horses, and carousel imagery conjured a warped carnival atmosphere. When vocalist コナリシグレ (Konari Shigure) opened with "Let's begin," five figures materialized—each with a distinct visual and sonic identity. The group's secret weapon became immediately apparent: 蔡厄ノ髑 (Saiyaku no Doku) commands the stage with an unsettling shriek that sits somewhere between deathcore brutality and industrial noise, while the interplay between Shigure's crystalline vocals and 夢喰イ獏 (Yume Kui Ibaku)'s menacing growl creates a deliberately chaotic stereo assault.

![Zetsubou Kinema performing on stage](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnew-img.barks.jp%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F11%2F20223043%2FIMG_5816.jpeg&w=800&output=jpg)

Tracks like "My Butterfly Delusion" and "My Illness is Anxiety (Kept)" showcased the group's gift for balancing deeply catchy melodies with genuinely disturbing lyrical content—a tonal tightrope most bands would fall from. The addition of stagecraft (dialogue, projection mapping, choreography) transformed each song into a mini-narrative rather than a performance interlude.

What makes Zetsubou Kinema resonate with modern Visual Kei audiences isn't nostalgia but synthesis: they're successfully bridging early 2000s "Neo-Visual" aesthetics (the eroguro-nonsense lineage of Malice Mizer andesque) with contemporary "yandere" and "damaged girl" cultural subtext—grounding it all in genuinely complex musicianship and five voices that refuse to blend into anonymity.

The setlist spanned 15 songs across the main set and encore, closing with "Zetsubou Kinema Circus," their statement of purpose. The announcement of a one-year anniversary show on December 21 at Nagoya's CLUB QUATTRO sent the crowd into euphoria.
