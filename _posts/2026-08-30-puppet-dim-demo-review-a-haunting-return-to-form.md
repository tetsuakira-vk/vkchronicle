When a band chooses to release a demo in 2026, it's rarely an accident—and Puppet†Dim's decision to return with DEMO feels deliberately provocative. After years of relative silence, this project arrives as both a statement and a question: what happens when a Visual Kei act strips away production polish and returns to raw fundamentals? The answer is unsettling, ambitious, and utterly essential listening.

DEMO captures Puppet†Dim in transition. Rather than a polished comeback vehicle, this feels like a window into the band's creative laboratory—a space where their signature gothic sensibilities collide with experimental impulses that suggest genuine artistic restlessness. The production aesthetic mirrors this tension: instrumentation is clear enough to parse, yet deliberately unrefined in ways that enhance rather than diminish the material. It's a calculated choice that separates genuine artistic vision from budget limitations, and Puppet†Dim navigates this distinction with considerable skill.

What strikes immediately is the band's refusal to rely on nostalgia. Yes, the DNA of their earlier work echoes throughout—that characteristic blend of orchestral darkness and heavy guitar work remains the foundation—but DEMO pushes into territory that feels genuinely forward-thinking for the Visual Kei scene. The vocal approach seems particularly evolved, moving between whispered intimacy and cathartic intensity in ways that suggest renewed creative confidence.

The demo format itself becomes thematically relevant. In an era of algorithmic playlists and streaming-optimized album structures, a demo feels like an act of resistance—a return to the underground ethos that once defined Visual Kei's countercultural edge. Puppet†Dim seems keenly aware of this irony, leaning into it rather than fighting it.

If there's a criticism to level, it's that occasional moments feel slightly unfocused—the experimental ambitions occasionally outpace the execution. But these instances are rare enough not to derail the listening experience, and they often feel intentional rather than accidental.

DEMO matters because it proves that Visual Kei's most challenging artists haven't abandoned their craft. In a landscape increasingly dominated by genre pastiche and retro-revivalism, Puppet†Dim's willingness to create something genuinely unsettling—something that resists easy categorization—feels necessary. This isn't a comeback album in the traditional sense; it's a reset button pressed with purpose.

For longtime fans, DEMO is essential. For newcomers curious about Visual Kei's experimental edges, it's an excellent entry point. Approach it expecting darkness, complexity, and genuine artistry—you'll find all three in abundance.

RECOMMENDATION: Highly recommended for adventurous listeners and devoted fans alike.

**Rating: 8/10**
