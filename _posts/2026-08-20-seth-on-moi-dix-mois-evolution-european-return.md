Moi dix Mois frontman Seth sat down recently to discuss the band's artistic direction, his reconnection with French audiences, and how visual kei continues to transform on the global stage.

For those unfamiliar, Moi dix Mois emerged from the French visual kei underground in the early 2000s as a vehicle for Seth's darkly poetic vision. Built on gothic architecture and theatrical intensity, the project has remained a fixture of the international VK circuit, blending orchestral arrangements with heavy guitar work and Seth's commanding vocal presence. The band occupies a unique space in the scene—rooted in classic VK aesthetics but never bound by genre convention.

During B7KLAN J-ROCK FESTIVAL, Seth opened up about what defines Moi dix Mois' universe and how it has evolved over the years. The conversation touched on the band's relationship with European audiences, particularly in France, where Moi dix Mois has maintained a dedicated following despite the geographical distance from Japan's VK epicenter. This return to European stages feels significant for a project that has always straddled East and West sensibilities.

Perhaps most intriguingly, Seth reflected on visual kei's broader evolution—how the movement has matured, diversified, and adapted to new generations of fans without losing its theatrical DNA. It's a conversation that matters beyond Moi dix Mois alone; it speaks to where the scene is heading and why artists like Seth continue to invest in the form's possibilities.

The special bonds connecting Seth and the band to Europe—cultural, artistic, and personal—emerged as a central theme. These ties aren't merely sentimental; they inform how Moi dix Mois approaches songwriting, live presentation, and their wider artistic mission.
