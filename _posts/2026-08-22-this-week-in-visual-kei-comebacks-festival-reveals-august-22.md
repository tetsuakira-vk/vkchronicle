It's been a massive week for Visual Kei, folks. We're talking band reunions, long-awaited comebacks, and some seriously stacked festival lineups—the kind of news cycle that reminds you why this scene never stops moving.

Let's start with the comebacks, because they've been *loud* this week. NON's return from retirement with a new solo project has fans in a frenzy, and rightfully so. That's the kind of legacy artist moment that doesn't come around often. Meanwhile, Sadie are back with "Flower of Evil" and a September release date locked in, while Jubaku Hyourei has regrouped as a full band after their hiatus. If you've been holding onto hope for any of these acts, this is your week to celebrate.

On the live event front, things are heating up. FEST. INAZUMA 2026 just dropped its final lineup, and Visual Kei has a solid presence: T.M.Revolution, OWV, ICEx, and abingdon boys school are all confirmed. Elsewhere, Godfrost is hosting a darker affair with Italian industrial act LITTLE BOYS, and the MASKED BEYOND event is bridging the gap between Visual Kei and J-Rock in Tokyo. If you're touring Japan or planning ahead, there's genuinely something for every flavor of fan.

New music is flowing steadily too. GLAY announced their 64th single "Dirty Hero" with an anime tie-in, sumika dropped "Umm" and launched the 'Flügel Letter' tour, and sugar have locked in their 6th single Amagami for November. yutori are taking the two-man route with "対 vol.2" featuring five guest bands—that's the kind of collaboration energy we live for. Plus, T.M.Revolution just released THE FIRST TAKE versions of classic hits, which is a beautiful way to revisit the catalog.

Interview-wise, Seth opened up about Moi dix Mois' evolution and their European return, giving us some real insight into how one of Visual Kei's most experimental acts continues to push boundaries.

And then there's the future: Quattro Cantare announced their debut live for 2027 with a star-studded guest lineup, and Petit Brabancon launched a five-month bootleg series with unreleased tracks. It's the kind of content pipeline that keeps the community fed.

This has been the week where Visual Kei proved it's not living on nostalgia alone—old guard acts are returning, new projects are launching, and the festival circuit is stacked.

Next week, we'll be watching for tour date announcements and which international acts might crack the Japanese festival circuit.
