GODLAND has officially closed the curtain on its inaugural tour, capping off the "Darkland Tour 2026" with a landmark performance at Zepp Shinjuku. The project—a collaboration between visual rock veteran MORRIE and BAKI, the former vocalist of cult act GASTUNK—delivered a statement finale that underscored the unit's ambitious creative vision.

For those unfamiliar with GODLAND, this is a fascinating collision of two underground Japanese rock worlds. MORRIE's storied career spans decades of boundary-pushing visual rock, while BAKI brings an outsider's edge from the experimental punk-rock realm. Together, they've crafted something that refuses easy categorization—a theatrical, sonically provocative project that demands attention from fans of avant-garde Japanese rock aesthetics.

The Zepp Shinjuku show wasn't a solo affair. Support came from a stellar lineup reflecting the project's experimental credentials: Ueno Koji (a respected figure in Japan's underground rock scene), shuji from Janne Da Arc (the legendary visual rock band known for their fusion of traditional Japanese elements and gothic theatricality), and SUGIZO, whose virtuosic guitar work has defined the sound of multiple generations of Japanese rock.

This kind of lineup speaks volumes about the respect GODLAND has garnered in such a short time. These aren't placeholder openers—they're collaborators and peers, each bringing their own uncompromising artistic vision to the bill.

The "Darkland Tour 2026" served as proof of concept for this unlikely partnership. For a debut tour to command a venue like Zepp Shinjuku and attract such caliber talent suggests GODLAND has tapped into something the Japanese rock underground was hungry for: unapologetic theatricality paired with genuine musical innovation.

For Western fans who've followed MORRIE's work or maintained interest in Japan's visual rock underground, GODLAND represents a project worth tracking closely. If you haven't caught wind of them yet, now's the time to dig in. Check out GODLAND's official channels and social media for news on any future releases or live announcements—this story is far from over.
