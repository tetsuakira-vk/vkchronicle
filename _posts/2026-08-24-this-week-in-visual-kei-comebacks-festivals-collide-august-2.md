It's been an absolute *week* in Visual Kei, folks. We're talking reunions, surprise projects, major festival confirmations, and enough tour announcements to keep your calendar permanently booked through 2026. The scene is firing on all cylinders right now.

Let's start with the comeback energy. NON's return from retirement with a new solo project has fans buzzing about what 2000s VK legends might be planning next. Meanwhile, Sadie are back with "Flower of Evil," dropping in September—a welcome return for anyone who's missed their sound. And if mystery projects are your thing, keep your eyes on タマタマ(仮), the egg-disguised newcomers who just emerged from nowhere with serious intrigue behind them.

On the live front, this week was festival central. FEST. INAZUMA 2026 just revealed its final lineup, and the Visual Kei representation is *strong*: T.M.Revolution, OWV, ICEx, and abingdon boys school all confirmed. Elsewhere, HERO and BabyKingdom are hosting the "Rengeki" festival on December 16, while Godfrost's dark collaboration with Italian duo LITTLE BOYS promises something delightfully niche. And we can't forget the historic two-man show between D'ESPAIRSRAY and KIRITO—watching these two worlds collide was genuinely special.

The release calendar is equally stacked. vistlip are moving forward with [Party On] -Deluxe- alongside holiday shows. yutori just announced their "対 vol.2" two-man tour with five supporting acts, and sumika dropped "Umm" while launching the 'Flügel Letter' tour. GLAY, ever the institution, are taking the anime tie-in route with their 64th single "Dirty Hero." Petit Brabancon, meanwhile, is going deep with a 5-month bootleg series featuring unreleased tracks—proper fan service.

We also got some unexpected crossover moments this week. The MASKED BEYOND event is bridging Visual Kei and J-Rock territories in Tokyo, while 新しい学校のリーダーズ ventured into airport campaign territory, blending VK aesthetics with mainstream visibility. And Seth's interview about Moi dix Mois' European return gave us serious insight into how Japanese VK is being received internationally.

It's the kind of week that reminds you why this community never stops moving. There's genuinely something for everyone right now—whether you're chasing underground mystery projects, camping festival lineups, or diving into legacy act deep cuts.

Next week, keep an eye on early September release announcements and any festival lineups still trickling in before autumn kicks into high gear.
