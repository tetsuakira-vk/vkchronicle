After a quarter-century anchoring the visual kei landscape, NIGHTMARE refuses to fade into nostalgia. *Kyokuyakou* arrives as proof that the Sendai legends remain a vital force—not resting on the laurels of "The World" and "Alumina," but pushing forward with the restless energy that defined their peak years.

The single signals a band recalibrating rather than retreading. NIGHTMARE's core lineup—Yomi's commanding vocals, Sakito's sculptural guitar work, and the locked rhythm section of Hitsugi and Ni~ya—sounds energized, operating with a clarity and purpose that feels deliberately contemporary. There's no desperation to chase trends, but rather a confidence in their own sonic vocabulary applied with fresh intensity.

*Kyokuyakou* demonstrates the band's evolving approach to melodicism and dynamic tension. Where their mid-2000s material married accessibility with darkness, this single leans into architectural sophistication—building passages that reward close listening while maintaining the visceral impact visual kei demands. Yomi's performance is particularly noteworthy, balancing vulnerability and control in a way that reminds longtime fans why his presence has been non-negotiable to the band's identity.

What's most striking is the refusal to coast. Many visual kei acts of NIGHTMARE's stature have settled into heritage status, touring the same setlists to dwindling audiences. Instead, this single reads as a statement: we're still interested in the present moment, still willing to take risks within our sound.

That said, a single can only reveal so much. *Kyokuyakou* hints at a broader artistic direction without fully committing to a full album's narrative arc. It's tantalizing but not conclusive—which is precisely what a strong single should be.

For longtime devotees and newcomers curious about visual kei's staying power, *Kyokuyakou* is essential listening. NIGHTMARE has earned the right to their legacy. More importantly, they've earned the right to move beyond it.

RECOMMENDATION: Essential for VK fans. Strong entry point for new listeners curious about the genre's luminaries.

**Rating: 8/10**
