When a band resurrects itself after a decade away, the real test isn't whether they can play the hits—it's whether they can still grow. Kuroyume proved they're doing exactly that on October 30th, delivering a landmark show at Tokyo's Line Cube Shibuya that doubled as both a birthday celebration for vocalist Kiyoharu and a sneak preview of where the band heads next.

Since their dramatic reunion in February 2025, Kuroyume has been on a creative tear. This year alone brought two reworked albums—CORKSCREW 2026 and Drug Treatment 2026—plus an ambitious three-night arena run at Toyota Arena Tokyo. But this particular show felt different. The setlist was bold in a way that suggested the band isn't just reliving past glories; they're actively reshaping their sound for a new era.

![Kuroyume on stage performing](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnew-img.barks.jp%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F11%2F02151117%2F%25E7%259F%25B3%25E4%25BA%2595%25E9%25BA%25BB%25E6%259C%25A8_20261030_210422_D5D_0083-1024x682.jpg&w=800&output=jpg)

The night opened with explosive energy—"FAKE STAR" and "DRIVE" established immediate command of the room—but the real revelation came when Kuroyume introduced two special collaborators: pianist Elena Kato and saxophonist Takeshi Kurihara. These aren't random guests. Both have been integral to Kiyoharu's recent solo work, and their integration here signals something significant: the band is deliberately expanding its sonic palette. When "Nemurenai Hi ni Miru Tokei," a track absent from live setlists for nearly thirty years, emerged draped in elegant piano arrangements and double vocals between Kiyoharu and bassist Hitoshi, it became clear this wasn't nostalgia—it was reimagining.

![Band performing with added musicians](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnew-img.barks.jp%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F11%2F02151147%2F261030_mori_0229-1024x683.jpg&w=800&output=jpg)

The magic extended to fan favorites. "MARIA" received a fresh arrangement that proved even beloved standards can surprise longtime listeners. Even the technical difficulties—audio monitor problems that prompted Kiyoharu to speak candidly about his standards as a performer—underscored the band's uncompromising approach. This wasn't a celebration willing to settle for "good enough."

![Kiyoharu performing vocals](https://images.weserv.nl/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnew-img.barks.jp%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2026%2F11%2F02151402%2F%25E9%259C%2591%25EF%25BD%25BD%25E8%259C%2589%25EF%25A3%25B0%25E7%25B9%25A7%25EF%25BD%25BB%25E7%25B9%259D%25EF%25BD%25AC%25E7%25B9%25A7%25EF%25BD%25AF%25E7%25B9%259D%25E3%2583%25BB261030_mori_0146-1024x683.jpg&w=800&output=jpg)

Most crucially, Kiyoharu announced that both Kato and Kurihara will make partial appearances on the upcoming Kuroyume 2027 Zepp Tour, launching in February. That's not a one-off collaboration—that's the band's future taking shape in real time.
