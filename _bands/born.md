BORN emerged from Tokyo's underground in 2007 as D&L, a unit forged by former members of RENNY AMY determined to push visual kei into more abrasive, punk-influenced territory. The quartet—vocalist Ryoga, guitarists K and Ray, and drummer TOMO—officially adopted the name BORN in April 2008, marking their debut live at Takadanobaba AREA. By June 2010, their signing to indie label PSC solidified their presence within the scene, and they capitalized on this momentum with a creative explosion that would define their career.

BORN's sonic identity crystallized across a rapid succession of releases that showcased their evolution from raw experimentalism to refined extremity. Their debut full-length, -Abnormal Head Machine- (2009), established the foundation, but the 2010 trilogy—BLACK BORN MARKET, BLACK THEORY MANIA, and BLACK DEAD MUZIC—announced a band unafraid to weaponize dissonance and chaotic energy. Albums like DOGMA (2011) and VIGOUR (2012) refined this aesthetic without softening its edges, while THE STALIN -666- (2014) and SUICIDAL MARKET ~Doze of Hope~ (2016) demonstrated their commitment to thematic darkness. Singles such as ProudiA (which reached #5 on the Oricon indies chart), DEMONS, and Psycho Diva became touchstones for fans seeking visual kei that married visual spectacle with genuinely transgressive musical ambition.

The band's cultural significance lay in their refusal to coast within established visual kei conventions. Their appearance on hide TRIBUTE III -Visual SPIRITS- (2013) positioned them as inheritors of that legacy's uncompromising spirit, while their European bootleg release signaled growing international recognition. BORN represented a particular lineage within VK—one that valued sonic experimentation and punk-influenced aggression over aesthetic polish alone.

After eight years of relentless creative output, BORN disbanded on May 26, 2016, following their final oneman performance, "SUPER BLACK MARKET," at Zepp DiverCity Tokyo. The decision marked the end of a brief but intensely productive era. Their legacy endures among fans who discovered in BORN a band that treated visual kei not as a stylistic checkbox but as a genuine vehicle for artistic extremity—proof that the genre's most vital moments often come from those willing to challenge its own traditions.
